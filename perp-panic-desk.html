      How this market works
      • One SUI perpetual for 24 hours, in 15-minute steps. Funding is paid every hour. It follows the public Perpetual Protocol v1 design, plus an automatic repeg rule.
      • The vAMM is x·y = k with $60M of virtual USDC. It holds no real money: its price moves only when someone trades. The oracle is the index price; in most scenarios it equals the exchange price.
      • Margin: up to 10× (10% initial margin). A position is liquidatable below a 6.25% margin ratio, measured on the vAMM price. When the vAMM and oracle are more than 10% apart, Perp v1 also checks the oracle and uses whichever is better for the trader.
      • Liquidation: above a 1.25% margin ratio a keeper closes 25% of the position; below that, all of it. The penalty is 1.25% of what is closed, half to the keeper and half to the insurance fund. On a full liquidation, leftover margin goes to the insurance fund.
      • Funding: every hour, (average vAMM price − average oracle price over the hour) ÷ 24, per vSUI. When longs and shorts don’t cancel, the insurance fund keeps or pays the difference.
      • Fees: 0.1% of the position on every open and close, paid to the insurance fund.
      • Payouts: winners are paid from the vault, which holds everyone’s margin. The insurance fund covers bad debt and shortfalls; once it is empty, the vault holds less than traders are owed.
      • Repeg: after three funding hours in a row with the vAMM more than 10% from the oracle, the protocol moves it to the oracle ± 5%, keeping k. The insurance fund pays or collects the cost. If that costs more than the fund holds, k is cut by 1% instead.
      • Keeper bots look every 15 minutes, so a position below 6.25% is liquidated on their next pass. Real keepers race each other; here you can beat them.
      • An arbitrage desk trades the vAMM toward the oracle each step; how hard it trades depends on the scenario.
      • Staking in the insurance fund isn’t part of Perp v1, whose fund belonged to the protocol. It is here to show who absorbs bad debt. Real staking usually has a withdrawal cooldown.
