Systemic risk

Lending Crash Chain

One lending market hit by everything at once: a price crash, a lagging oracle, LPs leaving the DEX, and a liquid staking token losing its peg.

  • Liquidators act only when selling the collateral pays, so a thin DEX stalls them
  • Holders of the staking token choose between selling at a discount and waiting to redeem
  • What-if runs (perfect oracle, deep DEX, no depeg) show which cause created the bad debt

Checked: each what-if run reuses the same price path and changes one cause at a time, so a difference in bad debt comes from that cause alone.

Real-time game

Panic Desk

You get $10,000 and two hours of market in real time, with headlines that may be false. Trade, lend, borrow, stake, provide liquidity or liquidate others.

  • Ten scenarios, from a quiet day and a rally to an oracle outage, an LST depeg, a lender run and a pump and dump
  • Before you start, it plays six standard strategies on the same market and shows what goes wrong for each
  • The debrief attributes your final result to each move you made, and the impacts add up to the total

Checked: replaying your moves reproduces the live run exactly; the strategy table and debrief use that same replay.

Perp Panic Desk

You get $10,000 and 24 hours of a SUI perpetual on a vAMM, in the style of Perpetual Protocol v1. Go long or short up to 10×, stake in the insurance fund, or liquidate others as a keeper.

  • Eight scenarios, from crowded longs bleeding funding and long and short squeezes to a slow oracle, a gap crash that empties the insurance fund, and a repeg
  • Before you start, it runs seven positions on the same market (2× and 10× longs and shorts, an insurance fund stake, a keeper) and shows what goes wrong for each
  • A crowd of leveraged traders, an arbitrage desk and keeper bots move the vAMM, so cascades and overshoots come out of the rules rather than a script

Checked: cash is conserved to the cent in every scenario, with and without your trades; each scenario's lesson holds across random seeds.

Guided lessons

Perp Workbench

A vAMM perpetual in the style of Perpetual Protocol v1, one step at a time: the vAMM, funding, keepers, liquidation, bad debt and repegs, with every USDC movement in a ledger.

  • Eleven guided lessons, from "who pays a winner" to a flash crash draining the insurance fund
  • Classic vAMM failure modes: front-running a repeg, an insurance fund that runs dry, a lagging oracle, a lopsided gap check and a flash crash
  • Free play: open and close positions yourself and watch the ledger

Checked: rules taken from the public Perpetual Protocol v1 ClearingHouse and Amm source; total USDC is conserved to the cent in every lesson.

How they were built

Each lab came out of a conversation with a coding agent while I prepared for a DeFi security role. I decided what each one had to show and reviewed every result. The agent wrote and tested the code.

  1. Set the questionFor example: "show me what happens to my $10,000, from the point of view of every user".
  2. Ground it in real codeThe agent read the public Perpetual Protocol v1 contracts before modeling funding, liquidation and repegs, so the rules match the source rather than a summary.
  3. Keep the math deterministicEvery number comes from plain JavaScript simulations, not from the model's text. The same inputs always give the same run.
  4. Test what must always be trueMoney is conserved, ledgers balance, replays match live runs, and pages work at desktop and phone widths in a headless browser.
  5. Review and push backI read the outputs as a user and as an auditor. Several bugs were only caught that way (below).
Where the agent helped mostReading unfamiliar protocol code quickly, turning a mechanism into a working model, and writing the many small tests a model needs.
Where judgment had to stay humanDeciding what each simulation should prove, spotting results that looked right but weren't, and deciding which simplifications were acceptable.
